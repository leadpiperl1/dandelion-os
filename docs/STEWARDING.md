# Stewarding the Dandelion OS commons

Dandelion OS is a public project shaped by its authors, contributors, and community. This page is a practical companion to the project's ethos—not a replacement for it. [`ACCESSIBILITY.md`](../ACCESSIBILITY.md) remains the primary statement of that ethos and should be read first.

## What stewardship means here

Stewardship is care for the work and the people who encounter it. It does not mean taking ownership away from the authors or speaking on behalf of maintainers. It means helping the project stay understandable, welcoming, honest, and useful while respecting its history and licenses.

## Shared commitments

- **Keep accessibility foundational.** Treat sensory, motor, and cognitive accessibility as a design concern from the beginning. Do not reduce the commitments in `ACCESSIBILITY.md` to a feature checklist or marketing claim.
- **Preserve the project's ideas and history.** Improve clarity without silently deleting concepts, terminology, or context. When a change supersedes an earlier approach, explain the transition and retain useful historical records.
- **Be precise about evidence.** Distinguish implemented behavior from proposals, simulations, measurements, and hardware-dependent results. State what was tested, how, and in what environment; do not present targets as verified outcomes.
- **Keep version records honest.** A version in `VERSION_LOG.md`, a Git tag, and a published GitHub Release are different records. Link them when they match, and say plainly when one is missing. Do not invent release history or retag published versions to make the page look uniform.
- **Make contribution approachable.** Use clear language, small reviewable changes, working links, and kind explanations. Welcome questions from newcomers and do not assume familiarity with GitHub or the project's specialized vocabulary.
- **Respect people and their agency.** Follow the [`Code of Conduct`](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), protect private information, and avoid treating disabled people as abstractions or making claims on their behalf.
- **Use the project's license and credit responsibly.** Preserve required notices and attribution, and consult the repository license when copying or adapting code and documentation.

## A careful path for improvements

1. Read [`ACCESSIBILITY.md`](../ACCESSIBILITY.md) and [`docs/START_HERE.md`](START_HERE.md).
2. Identify whether the change is documentation, implementation, a proposal, or a release record.
3. Keep the patch focused and preserve context that readers need to understand the project’s evolution.
4. Check links, accessibility of the documentation itself, and any relevant build or verification commands.
5. In the change description, explain the intent, what changed, what did not change, and what was checked.
6. Invite review and be open to correction. Stewardship is collaborative, not a claim of infallibility.

## Release-page care

Before publishing a GitHub Release, confirm the intended tag and target commit, review the notes for accuracy, and attach only artifacts that are ready to share. Explain which project epoch or milestone the release represents. Preserve older release records; if a record is confusing, clarify its notes rather than rewriting the project's history without a clear reason.

For current release status, compare the [GitHub Releases page](https://github.com/leadpiperl1/dandelion-os/releases), repository tags, [`VERSION_LOG.md`](../VERSION_LOG.md), and [`CHANGELOG.md`](../CHANGELOG.md). Those sources serve related but distinct purposes.
